Pipe Stats

Live

A searchable record of competitive piping and drumming results.

Pipe Stats collects the published prize lists of the associations that run the Highland-games circuit and builds a career profile for every piper, drummer, band, judge and venue. I designed it, built it and run it, including the data pipeline, database, site and admin tools.

  • Search competitors, bands, judges and venues, each with a full results history
  • Standings and records by season and grade, plus all-time wins, streaks and milestones
  • A 3D globe of contests and venues
  • Profiles you can claim, follow and correct, and a form to submit missing results
  • Pipedle and PipeMaster, built into the site (below)

PipeMaster

A sheet-music editor for the Highland bagpipe. Write a tune by tapping notes, add embellishments with a key press and play it back with drones. It imports Bagpipe Music Writer and PiobMaster files, exports MusicXML, MIDI and PDF, and can read printed music from a photo or PDF.

Open PipeMaster Open-source original

pipestats.app/pipedle

Pipedle

A daily word puzzle about piping, drumming and the Highland games. You get six guesses and a category hint, and every answer comes with a short explanation, so it teaches the vocabulary as you play. Signed-in players get streaks, stats and a daily leaderboard.

02

Fuel Quest

Private

A nutrition and training-habit app that works like a game.

Meals, supplements, water, workouts and meal prep are quests that earn XP. Levels, streaks and achievements keep an Ironman training block on track. It is a PWA, so it installs on a phone like a native app.

  • Quests for meals, supplements, water, workouts and Sunday meal prep
  • Levels, streaks and ten achievements, from Beginner Athlete up to Ironman
  • AI macro estimates from a text description or a photo of the meal
  • An adaptive calorie target learned from your weight trend
  • Garmin recovery data (sleep, HRV, resting heart rate) and Strava workouts
  • Push reminders and passkey sign-in

Built with Next.js 16, TypeScript, Tailwind 4, Supabase, PWA

Not publicly accessible. These are the real screens running on demo data (“Demo Athlete”), not my personal logs.

Training Dashboard

Private

A training cockpit built on my Strava data.

A dashboard for my swim, bike and run training. It counts down to race day, follows a 52-week plan from sprint triathlon to IRONMAN 70.3, and turns raw activities into fitness, fatigue and load figures.

  • Race countdown, weekly goal and week-over-week comparison
  • Fitness and fatigue (CTL and ATL), training load, heart-rate zones, pace distribution
  • Every activity in a calendar heatmap, with sport filters, per-mile splits and route maps
  • A 52-week plan with a swipeable day view
  • Locked down: Strava credentials stay on the server, and sign-in is by passkey or password

Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind, Vercel Edge Functions, Strava API, WebAuthn

Not publicly accessible. Screenshots use generated demo data, not my real training.

04

Phish or Legit?

Live

Two login pages that look identical. One is a phishing site.

A browser game that shows why device-bound passkeys resist phishing. You pick the real site from two look-alikes, and the result shows exactly what was different. You can then register a real passkey and watch your browser refuse the fake site.

  • 39 scenarios across a dozen techniques, from typosquats to homoglyphs, with fictional brands
  • Explains each answer: the tell is highlighted, and look-alike domains show their real punycode
  • Real WebAuthn: create a passkey on your device and test it against the imposter
  • Plain JavaScript with no dependencies, deployable anywhere

SecTor 2026 report

Live

Conference notes, written up as a report.

My notes from SecTor 2026 in Toronto (October 6–8): a six-minute executive brief, session pages with sources, a glossary, short audio recaps and a 50-question challenge. It is a static site with no build step and no dependencies.

  • Brief first, then day-by-day session pages with a provenance badge on each claim
  • A 50-question challenge with category scores, and a glossary linked to the sessions that used each term
  • Three reading styles (Terminal, Broadsheet, Plain), a report theme, and light and dark modes
  • Short narrated recaps for each day

Personal conference notes. Not an official CIBC publication or position.
